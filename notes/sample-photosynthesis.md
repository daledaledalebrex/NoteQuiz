# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It
happens in two linked stages: the light-dependent reactions in the thylakoid
membranes, and the Calvin cycle in the stroma of the chloroplast.

In the light-dependent reactions, photosystem II absorbs photons and splits
water. This releases oxygen as a by-product, supplies replacement electrons,
and pumps protons into the thylakoid lumen. The resulting proton gradient
drives ATP synthase. Photosystem I then re-energises the electrons to reduce
NADP+ into NADPH.

The Calvin cycle uses that ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide. The enzyme
RuBisCO attaches CO2 to ribulose bisphosphate, and the resulting molecules are
reduced to G3P. Most G3P regenerates RuBP so the cycle can continue; the rest
is exported to build glucose and other sugars.

RuBisCO can also bind oxygen instead of carbon dioxide, a wasteful process
called photorespiration. C4 and CAM plants avoid it by concentrating CO2
near RuBisCO, either spatially in bundle sheath cells or temporally by
fixing CO2 at night.
